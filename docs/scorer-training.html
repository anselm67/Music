Scorer Training Log

End-to-end model: detection (staffer) + transcription (noter) in a single forward pass.

The scorer is a two-stage model trained jointly. The staffer half (ViT backbone + HierarchicalDecoder) predicts stave bounding boxes; a differentiable grid_sample crop bridge extracts per-stave image patches at those predicted coordinates; the noter half (encoder-decoder Transformer) transcribes each crop into a Kern token sequence. All weights are updated end-to-end through both losses. Dataset: PDMX subset with ≤2 staves/system (System2.csv). Validation runs multiple times per epoch; charts show the last checkpoint of each epoch.

Baseline standalone models: staffer stave-primary-grid-full (sys IoU 0.985, stave_err 0.41 px) and noter enhanced3 (99.58% token accuracy).

All runs

merge1 trains on PDMX (System2.csv); boulez & walton fine-tune on KernSheet (real scans). For the KernSheet runs the Val acc / Sys IoU / Stave err columns are on independent, unseeded validation splits and are not directly comparable run-to-run — the matched 300-page eval (identical pages for both models) is the honest comparator.

merge1

2026-06-02 · tag: train/merge1 · 8 epochs · initialized from stave-primary-grid-full + enhanced3

First end-to-end training run. Both halves are initialized from the best standalone checkpoints, then fine-tuned jointly. The staffer loss (L1 + GIoU + objectness) and noter loss (cross-entropy over token sequences) are summed and back-propagated in one pass through the differentiable crop bridge.

The training shows a characteristic early dip: at ep0 the model inherits strong standalone scores (sys IoU ~0.89 by end of epoch), but ep1–ep2 see a temporary regression as the model adapts to the joint gradient signal. Recovery is steady from ep3 onward, and by ep7 all metrics are still improving — the run is not fully converged.

boulez, walton & ravel — KernSheet fine-tune

2026-06-04 · tags train/boulez / train/walton / train/ravel · from stave-primary-grid-full-kernsheet + enhanced3-center+jitter-kernsheet · shared 5543 vocab

First end-to-end runs on KernSheet (real scanned piano scores), rebuilt fresh from the two KernSheet-fine-tuned standalone branches at the shared 5543 vocab — no checkpoint-growing needed. Three runs answer two questions — should the detector be frozen? and does longer joint training help?:

~ boulez (8 ep) — staffer unfreezes at step 500 (freeze_staffer_steps=500, the merge1 default), then trains jointly. The detector keeps receiving its full detection loss for 6 more epochs.
~ walton (8 ep) — staffer frozen for the whole run (--freeze-staffer-steps 3000 > 2000 total steps). The noter adapts to predicted crops; the detector never moves. The freeze ablation.
~ ravel (20 ep) — boulez recipe, longer (boulez was still improving at ep7). Converged ~ep14.

Matched eval — identical 300 pages, all three models

The in-training Val acc / IoU / stave_err live on independent unseeded splits (walton's frozen stave_err is 4.53 px vs boulez's 3.86 px on the same frozen checkpoint — proof the splits differ), so they are not comparable. Analysis — one consistent story: detection training drives end-to-end

+ The matched eval rank-orders by how much the detector trained: walton (frozen) 87.2% → boulez (8 ep joint) 89.5% → ravel (20 ep joint) 90.7%. Miscount pages fall in lock-step: 32 → 12 → 3.
+ The lever is continued detection training: with λ_det=1, the unfrozen staffer keeps getting its full L1+GIoU+objectness loss, which improves recall/counting. Walton's frozen detector got none of it; ravel got the most.
− val/stave_err_px is a misleading proxy for detector quality: GT-routed edge-L1 on already-matched boxes, blind to recall. Joint training raised it (3.86→5.25 px boulez; ~5.5 px ravel) while improving the counting that actually drives transcription. Trust the matched eval, not stave_err_px.
~ The eval's top-to-bottom order matching over-penalizes a miscount (a missed/extra stave shifts every match below it), so the similarity spread is partly amplified by the miscount spread — but the underlying miscount gaps are real. (To be de-amplified once eval moves to center-y linear_sum_assignment.)
~ Caveat: this is "more detection fine-tuning helped," not proof the transcription→box gradient helped — isolating that needs a det-loss-only continued staffer fine-tune.
ravel is the KernSheet best — 90.7% matched similarity, only 3/300 miscount pages. The progression walton → boulez → ravel is monotonic in both similarity and miscount, all explained by how much the detector kept training. The pre-run hypothesis that freezing would protect the detector (because KernSheet boxes are a thirds approximation) was wrong: more joint detection training is what helps.

berlioz — the letterbox rebuild, end-to-end

2026-06-06 · tag train/berlioz · 20 ep · ravel recipe on a fully letterbox-retrained staffer+noter letterbox · wash

berlioz is the end-to-end verdict on the letterbox page transform (aspect-preserving resize + pad, replacing the independent-x/y stretch). The entire pipeline was rebuilt under it — PDMX base + KernSheet fine-tune for both branches (staffer/berlioz-kernsheet, noter/berlioz-kernsheet) — then merged and fine-tuned exactly as ravel (same 20-epoch recipe, only the transform differs).

End-to-end the letterbox is a wash — and not worth its cost. Transcription similarity is statistically indistinguishable from ravel (90.8% unseeded vs 90.7% page-matched — within the noise between two different 300-page draws), but berlioz miscounts more (6 vs 3 / 300), inheriting the weaker detector. The trade is now clear: un-stretching helps the noter at most marginally (val edge, free-running tie) and hurts the staffer measurably (~1.8 px softer on real scans), and the two cancel. ravel stays the KernSheet best. The letterbox is being reverted; if revisited, the right scope is the noter crop only, with a stretched (or otherwise canvas-filling) page for the detector.
